## Structural Pattern
The Mabinogion tales have a structural pattern that moves through conflict, exchange and alliance. The plot is usually shaped around a crisis breaking out at court, kinship ties being tested, or a meeting with another, "other" kingdom (mostly with supernatural forces stepping in). The characters' actions rest less on individual heroism than on the effort to secure the honor and unity of the social unit they stand for (clan or dynasty). (As stressed in the work of Ceridwen Lloyd-Morgan and Roberta Valente, in these patterns female figures usually work as the "object of exchange" at the center of the crisis, or as the one who resolves it.)

## Symbolic / Cosmological Frame (brief, details in Erendiz)
Elements in the tales such as magic fountains, shape-shifting beings and Annwn (the otherworld) are not just fantasy; they are symbolic barriers that draw the line between order (the human world/the court) and chaos (nature/the unknown). The bond of figures like Rhiannon with horses or the land is the remnant of an ancient myth of a sovereignty goddess. The supernatural stepping into the social order and the way heroes resolve these crises point to the restoring of a cosmological balance, but building out this dimension in depth is Erendiz's area.

## Social Function
This work justifies and passes on the political and social structure of medieval Welsh society:

- **Kinship and Institutional Responsibility:** Kinship is not just a biological tie but the primary mechanism of political alliance. Inheritance, the transfer of authority and alliances (through uncle-nephew relations or marriages) secure the stability of society. Blood feuds are treated as a collective, not individual, duty and directly affect the security of the kingdom.
- **Class Hierarchy and 'Sarhaed':** Society shows a hierarchical structure around the power of the elites (kings, princes, nobles). In the stories, rich feasts, gift-giving (hospitality) and the size of the retinue are conspicuous consumption of power and status. The system of 'sarhaed' (the price for insult/honor) works according to a person's class and gender. Breaches must be compensated; justice depends on restoring the honor of authority.
- **Women's Role in Alliance and the Transfer of Property:** In the branches of the text (especially Pwyll, Branwen and Math), women are the most strategic figures who resolve (or trigger) political tensions between dynasties. In the context of medieval Welsh law (Cyfraith Hywel), even though women could not own land, they had rights over movable property (cowyll, agweddi). But in the stories, the female body becomes political capital used to make peace:
  - *Branwen's Tragedy:* Branwen is given to Matholwch to join Wales and Ireland. But because of the *sarhaed* Efnisien creates, this political investment collapses. Branwen's story exposes the limits of using women only as objects of exchange, and how, because of men's whims (wounded honor), these alliances can turn into a bloody war that destroys both peoples.
  - *Political Expectations and Control of the Body:* Even though Rhiannon chooses Pwyll of her own will (agency), when she is seen to fail at her basic socio-political task of "producing an heir" (when her child is abducted), the system crushes her at once and sentences her to a symbolic slavery (carrying arriving guests like a horse). A woman's sovereign power is legitimate only as long as it serves patriarchal inheritance law.
  - *Blodeuwedd and the Rejection of the System:* The betrayal of Blodeuwedd, who has no natural kinship network and is created by magic only as a "property/wife" for Lleu, can be read as an ontological and social revolt against a woman being brought into existence merely as an object.
- **The Central Authority of the Court:** Courts are the institutional centers where hospitality, rituals and power are put on display, and where order is protected against wild nature.

## Linguistic Register (brief, details in Tyler)
The language and style of transmission carry traces of the passage from oral culture (cyfarwydd) to written tradition. The rhythm of this form of transmission shows how cultural memory is encoded. The structural details of narrative technique and formulas are left to Tyler's analysis.

## Pass 2 Note
Erendiz describing Annwn not as hierarchical but as "a neighboring sovereignty", and reading power as a functional protocol, proves that the authority of the elites rests less on a divine essence than on performing social duties (hospitality, contract). Tyler noticing that in the Manawyddan text "daily labor" is set against magic is a very valuable economic and class observation, pointing to the noble class using craft production as a mechanism for survival and for rebuilding order when the system collapses.

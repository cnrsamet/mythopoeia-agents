I tested this pass with these questions: Why is the hero kept at the center of the story for so long without his name being said? Through which shifts of narrator is the journey home turned into a story of knowing/recognizing? What do the repeated scenes of hosting, song and news speed up, and what do they delay? In which concrete signs is the tension between disguise and truth resolved? And last, why does the text return to talk and testing after the peak of violence?

The primary text is Samuel Butler's English prose translation. So I make no firm claims about meter or sound patterns in the English words; the study rests on repetitions, scene order, handovers between narrators and the logic of images, which can be followed in translation too.

## Structural Pattern

The poem does not start at the end of the return but inside the absence that longs for it. The nameless, many-sided man of the first lines only steps on stage in the fifth book. The first four books build instead Telemachus's house, Penelope's waiting, the noise of the suitors and the stories of distant warriors. So before he is seen, Odysseus becomes the weight of a name moving through other people's memory, not a person. The core tension of the first half is the question "where?"; the second half's is "when he comes, who will know him, and when?"

The structure moves through four thresholds: the search in which Telemachus finds his voice; Odysseus getting from Calypso to Phaeacia; telling his own journey for the first time in the first person at the Phaeacian palace; and the controlled unveiling in Ithaca, run in a beggar's disguise. So books nine to twelve are not just a flashback: Odysseus himself fills the gap the outer narrator leaves. After saying his own name, he builds himself as both the doer and the teller of the adventure through the Cyclops, Aeolus, Circe, the Dead and the herds of Helios. But once he reaches Ithaca, this reliable, grand voice gives way to invented stories of origin. The poem shows on stage that telling can hide the truth as much as it can reveal it.

The two halves mirror each other. In Phaeacia the stranger is first watched in silence, then opens up in front of a game and a song, and in the end is carried home by ship. In Ithaca too the stranger is first tested, is recognized by a small part of the household, and becomes visible to everyone only when he strings the bow. As academic narrative studies also stress, the recognitions are not scattered at random: the order Telemachus, Argos, Eurycleia, Eumaeus and Philoetius, Penelope and Laertes is private knowledge spreading from a narrow circle to a wide and dangerous stage. Every recognition raises the price of the next one.

Another pattern keeps circling the main story: the tale of Orestes and Aegisthus. This short example, started by Zeus in the assembly, is retold in different settings by Athena, Nestor, Menelaus and the dead Agamemnon. Retelling the same event tells the listener the suitors' end in advance, but for each new teller it does a different job: a model for Telemachus, a counter-image for Penelope, a dark possibility for Odysseus's return. The large plot is pulled tight by the echoes the small stories carry.

## Symbolic / Cosmological Frame (brief, details in Erendiz)

The sea is not just an obstacle, it is the story's machine for delay: every crossing from shore to shore suspends part of the identity. In contrast, Ithaca's smoke, the threshold stone, the bed and the orchard tree are things that stay in place and can be checked. The bed tied to a living olive tree in particular turns marriage from an abstract promise of faithfulness into a structure that cannot be pulled up. Athena's disguises and Poseidon's sea barrier drive this narrative contrast: the road opens, but the face does not show at once.

## Social Function (brief, details in Wilson)

The hosting scene is the poem's most used narrative measure: welcome at the door, water, food, seating, asking the name, and the gift. Each repetition of this sequence gives the listener a safe tempo. When the same pattern is broken by the suitors' endless feast, the breakage is visible without any sermon. The bards Phemius and Demodocus are also more than decoration for the feast; they decide which past the community can bear to hear. Penelope wanting to stop a song makes clear whose wound the story opens.

## Linguistic Register

The poem's voice works between an all-knowing narrator and speakers who test each other. The outer voice often announces the outcome ahead of time: it says in the first invocation that the companions will be ruined by touching the herds of Helios; it marks the suitors' end; it lays open to the reader the caution going through Odysseus's mind. In contrast, the words the characters say to each other are partial, indirect and often disguised. These two layers produce irony: the reader knows who the beggar is, Eumaeus does not; Eurycleia knows when she sees the scar but has to keep quiet; Penelope seems to know but keeps asking for proof.

The main verbs of this tension are asking, telling, hiding and testing. The repeated call to "tell me truly" in Butler's translation is not just a formal courtesy: when the stranger's name is asked and Odysseus invents a new past, the truth stands right beside it. Odysseus's power, even before battle, is often that he first controls the talk. Covering Anticlus's mouth inside the Wooden Horse; giving the Cyclops a false name first; getting Eumaeus to praise him; meeting Penelope's question with another story: these are the same technique at different scales. Here, speech is the waiting room of action.

Recognition scenes are the poem's most delicate tool of rhythm. The sign is never only visual: Argos's reaction is the bodily trace of a shared past; the scar showing up triggers a long flashback taking Eurycleia to Odysseus's youth; Penelope's test of the bed asks for knowledge of how it was made that belongs only to the husband; for Laertes, the scar is completed with the names of the trees in the orchard. So identity is not announced as "believe this face"; it is built by an object, a body and a shared memory confirming each other. Penelope's last test matters most: while Odysseus tests others with his mind, the poem draws final certainty from his involuntary, angry reaction. For a moment, control passes to the one being tested.

Formula and repetition do not make events mechanical; each time they give emotional ground. Dawn, preparing a meal, washing hands, launching a ship and the fixed transitions at the end of speeches create breathing space between scenes. Even in prose translation, "rosy-fingered Dawn" and the recurring sunrise turns make one feel that time moves on regardless of personal pain. In the same way, the detailed repetitions of hosting make the gap between Phaeacian generosity and the looting in Ithaca heard without a single explanation.

The images keep bringing great heroism down to the domestic and the bodily. Odysseus's heart turns this way and that like a paunch a man turns before a fire; Penelope stands by the door post with her veil; Odysseus clings to the shore from the sea like an octopus. These are not decoration: they close the distance between a warrior's resolve and a fragile, closed body. Part of the poem's staying power is that it ties a journey of world scale again and again to signs you can hold in your hand, such as smoke, bread, bed, scar and tree.

The song scenes are the narrative testing its own art. Phemius's song of return is unbearable to Penelope; Demodocus's songs of Troy turn Odysseus's hidden tears into body language. Odysseus then asks the bard to sing the Wooden Horse, and after that starts his own longer story. So the poem shows that storytelling both opens the wound and gives the wound a shape. If Odysseus's story in Phaeacia were truly a victory speech, the lies in Ithaca would weaken it; instead the text shows the narrator as a craftsman who adjusts voice and degree of truth to different listeners.

In the last part, stringing the bow is the hidden voice becoming visible through a public blow; but the poem does not end on this strong image. It returns to the bedroom, the rumor of the dead, Laertes's orchard and a final calming. This delayed ending says that revenge is not the narrative solution: the homecoming is only complete when speech can be shared again and signs can be read again. That is why the chain of recognitions in the second half does not only produce tension; it rebuilds the nameless hero in different voices as son, master, husband and father.

## Pass 2 Note

Erendiz reading xenia as a cosmic threshold and Wilson reading it as the social measure of civilization sharpens for me the narrative function of the repeated hosting scene. When the same ritual sequence is built as a savage reversal at the Cyclops, a flawless performance in Phaeacia and a repetition broken from inside in Ithaca, the listener hears the degree of order without waiting for an explanation. So the formula becomes, beyond a memory aid for oral storytelling, a rhythmic scale that tests the humanity of each community.

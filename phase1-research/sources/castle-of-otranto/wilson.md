## Structural Pattern
The story is built on a usurped and illegitimate power (Manfred) being shaken by a supernatural/historical intervention and giving way to the legitimate, blood-based rightful heir (Theodore). The most visible repeating motif is the mismatch between the giant, bodiless past (armor, helmet, sword, Alfonso's giant ghost) and the fragile, anxious power of the present. This motif keeps staging the belief that a power's physical strength cannot stand without moral and historical legitimacy. At the end of the novel, the giant ghost bringing down the castle and pointing to Theodore himself completes the restoration of the broken cosmic and political order.

## Symbolic / Cosmological Frame (brief, details in Erendiz)
The castle stops being an ordinary stone building and becomes a living, trapping ecosystem that holds "the sins of the ancestors". Supernatural punishing objects (the helmet falling from the sky) and giant ghosts breaking the order of the place signal how divine justice suspends worldly rules and physics. This ruin is the declaration that illegitimate rule (tyranny) is ontologically void.

## Social Function

The sub-questions that framed this reading were:
1. *Which anxieties in the fiction of property and nobility does Manfred's effort to legitimize his authority reflect?*
2. *How do the giant historical objects (helmet, armor) make social the noble class being crushed under its historical inheritance?*
3. *How does the inborn nobility of Theodore, thought to be a peasant within the class hierarchy, reproduce the myth of "the sanctity of blood"?*
4. *How does the border between the church and the castle model the conflict between worldly and spiritual authority?*

**1. Usurped Power and Patriarchal Anxiety over Lineage**
Manfred's power rests on force (and on his ancestor Ricardo's usurpation), so he keeps trying to ground this power through "a legitimate heir". The text shows how fragile and paranoid feudal authority can be without inborn rightfulness. His son (Conrad) being crushed under a giant helmet is not only a supernatural event; it also symbolizes made-up claims of nobility being literally "crushed" under the real (and heavy) historical inheritance. Manfred sees women (Hippolita, Isabella, Matilda) only as "tools of production and property" that can be used to continue his line and make land deals. His patriarchal tyranny is a social picture of how the fiction of passing property through blood turns into violence in a moment of crisis.

**2. The Sanctity of Blood and 18th-Century Whig Politics**
Theodore, who appears in the novel as a peasant youth, shows natural virtue, intelligence and courage against Manfred's brute force. But the story ties this virtue not to his being a peasant but to his "hidden nobility" (being the true heir), which is revealed later. At the end of the novel, Alfonso's giant vision bringing down the castle and blessing Theodore himself as "the true heir" confirms the text's strict conservative myth of class: "true virtue can only come from noble blood". Historically, this fiction reflects 18th-century English Whig politics (to which Walpole belonged) and the Glorious Revolution of 1688: an absolutist, corrupt and tyrannical king (Manfred) overthrown and replaced by the restoration of a legitimate, virtuous and "natural" rule (Theodore). The castle stands for the old despotic order on the verge of collapse, while the handover works like a divine constitution.

**3. Institutional Religion (the Church) against Worldly Authority**
The castle stands for worldly, tyrannical and lawless power (Manfred), while the church next door and Father Jerome stand for an independent institutional space that limits this power and can give refuge. Manfred killing his own daughter (Matilda) by mistake in the sacred space is the self-destroying peak of tyranny's lack of limits. Clergy being a moral counterweight to the power of the sword takes on the "check-and-balance" role religion plays in society. Manfred and his wife finally shutting themselves in the convent is the total submission of worldly tyranny before the spiritual institution.

## Linguistic Register (brief, details in Tyler)
The language of the text has a Gothic architecture of broken borders that builds feelings of the "sublime" and "terror". Class differences are clearly seen in the characters' dialogue: the sharp divide between the tragic gravity and elevated language of the nobles (fitting "Whig" virtue) and the comic, scattered speech of the servants keeps the social hierarchy in language too.

## Pass 2 Note

The "mismatch between justice (the return of the line) and human happiness" Erendiz found in the final scene is sociological proof of how the feudal and aristocratic structure makes the individual into a tool. The order completes the political restoration on the macro level to reproduce its own legitimacy (the line and property), but in the process it fully crushes the emotional reality of individuals. Theodore and Isabella being pushed into marriage for the sake of the political system repairing itself shows that the mechanism of power reduces the person to a mere means of transfer that keeps the structure standing.

## Round 1 Synthesis (2026-10-01, 6 new sources this round)

### Recurring Patterns

**1. Order is kept at thresholds, not built once.** In every source the cosmos is a balance kept alive by working a border correctly. In the Odyssey, xenia is a cosmic law under Zeus, tested again at every shore; the Cyclops is monstrous exactly because he has "neither assemblies nor laws". In the Mabinogion, Annwn is a neighboring kingdom reached by crossing the edge of a forest, and the only sin is crossing it rudely. In the Turkish tales the Div Mother is calmed with the right form of address ("little mother"), not with force. The threshold is the place where a world shows whether it holds.

**2. Death is never defeated, only moved, delayed or paid for.** Bran's cauldron returns bodies without speech (Mabinogion). Tuonela lets some return and not others, depending on how they died (Kalevala). The Youth Without Age tale turns immortality into a place, and the debt is collected the moment the hero longs for home (Turkish tales). Achilles would rather be a serf alive than a king among the dead (Odyssey). What lasts is the name or the story, not the body.

**3. Knowledge does not change fate, it only changes how fate is faced.** The Volva tells Othin his death and he cannot change it; Sigurth hears his whole future from Gripir and still goes (Poetic Edda). In Otranto the prophecy is read as a metaphor by everyone and comes true to the letter. Knowing is a way of standing in front of the end, not a way out of it.

**4. Wholeness that is locked up is dangerous; scattered, it becomes fertile.** The Sampo, owned by one power, breeds war; its shattered pieces seed the shore (Kalevala). Alfonso's body, scattered after a murder, must gather again and break the false castle (Otranto). The scattered life of the Wind-Div is its weakness, while the hero's single, unrepeatable life is his strength (Turkish tales).

### Usable Features for Worldbuilding

- **Origin-telling as power** (Kalevala): you control a thing by telling how it came to be. A world where healers, smiths or judges must know the "birth story" of what they handle.
- **The incomplete return** (Mabinogion): something can be brought back, but always missing one thing (speech, memory, warmth). A strong engine for loss without fantasy machinery.
- **Immortality as a place you cannot leave** (Turkish tales): the cost is not sin but longing. Works even in a world without magic, as a metaphor for exile or for a life that freezes the moment you look back.
- **The rooted proof** (Odyssey): the final proof of identity is an object that cannot be moved (the bed built on a living olive tree). Words can lie; a rooted thing cannot.
- **Justice and happiness as two currencies** (Otranto): an ending can close the account book of the world and still leave the people in it unhappy.

### Saturated Themes

- The threshold motif already appears in all six sources with the same mechanism (border, permeability, protocol, price). New sources will add local color, not new structure.
- "Knowing does not save" is consistent across the Edda, Otranto and the Odyssey; more sources of the same family will only repeat it.

### Open Areas / Gaps

- Five of six sources are European or Mediterranean; only the Turkish tales come from outside, and they reach us through two layers of translation. The patterns above may be one culture family repeating itself.
- No source where the sacred is quiet and domestic rather than heroic or royal. Everything sacred here sits in courts, tombs or seas.
- No source shows a cosmos that is simply indifferent; every universe here keeps accounts.

# Writer-Brain: story-1 (Saltreach)

Tyler's free-form notebook for this story. A new entry is added after every chapter, no old entry is ever deleted.

--------------------

## Chapter 1

Ada returns on the falling tide with a bookkeeper's need for exact times and complete accounts. The chapter establishes her perceptual grammar early: she watches the safe-water mark, counts the causeway posts, inventories the postbag, reads deterioration in Joss's handwriting, and finally enters her own clean line in the night-watch log. The movement is from public crossing to enclosed duty: causeway, post room, keeper's house, lantern room. By the end, the incoming tide has cut off the road and made her provisional one-month stay feel materially less provisional.

Mara carries the chapter's warmth and its first open emotional account. Her statement that anger and gladness can "both sit in the cart" gives the story a humane rule without excusing Ada's long absence. The onion biscuits and the argument over sorting bad-news letters keep ordinary life present beside estrangement. Mara also says Lena's name and supplies a small remembered judgment about Joss, opening the mother's presence without turning her into exposition.

Joss's failing hands are shown first through displaced details: the case striking his shin, blots beside the kitchen log, an overpressed figure eight, the ringing lamp glass. He finally admits the practical extent of the failure and, more importantly, says directly that he cannot keep the light alone. Ada's taking the scissors and then the watch is the chapter's irreversible action. Their shared joke over the inaccurate tower clock offers a narrow but genuine warmth between them.

The older logbooks appear only at the end. Ada notices that the volume for the year of the *Wren* is present, but she has no reason yet to open it; this preserves the later discovery while putting the physical evidence in the reader's field. Joss's contribution to Mara's ferry lamp is also planted as an unexplained habit of accounting for lights. Avoid explaining either gesture too early.

Open forward pressures: Ada has committed to the night watch and will need to negotiate the continuing division of work with Joss; Mara has named Ada's departure as a wound, but their friendship has begun to reopen; Ada has seen the relevant old logbook; Joss's quiet financial support of Mara's ferry remains unexplained. The central image for continuation is two hands in one record: Joss's once-clean script deteriorating under pressure, Ada's smaller figures beginning beside it.
